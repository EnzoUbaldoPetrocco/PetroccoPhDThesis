\begin{abstract}
In human's behavioral studies, cultural competence is the capacity of recognizing, understanding and adapting to diverse cultural contexts. This definition can be extended to systems such as social robots (SRs). Within this domain, the absence of such competence results in system malfunctions, decreased user satisfaction, and significant ethical concerns. Although this field has received increasing scholarly attention, several critical dimensions remain underexplored.
This thesis addresses the technical and ethical consequences of the underrepresentation of minority cultures in machine learning (ML) models integrated into SRs. Specifically, it investigates how culturally imbalanced datasets facilitate the development of culturally incompetent models—systems that fail to generalize or adapt effectively to minority groups. To mitigate these biases, this work evaluates strategies derived from multi-task learning and data preprocessing.
Furthermore, recognizing that cultural competence is frequently and narrowly framed through the lens of national culture—--a pragmatic but oversimplified approximation—--this work proposes and evaluates a novel architecture for cultural adaptation. This framework is designed to account for multifaceted cultural dimensions beyond nationality, addressing the current limitations in delegating such dynamic adaptation solely to standard machine learning models.
\end{abstract}
